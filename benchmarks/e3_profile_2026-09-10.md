# Where lossy e3 spends its time — 2026-09-10

`benchmarks/ladder_vs_cjxl_2026-09-10.*` put lossy **e3 at 1.40x cjxl v0.12 at
1024² threads=1 and 1.45x at 2048²** — the worst band on the ladder, and the
only one where the gap grows with image size (at 64² and 256² the same band is
0.88-1.01x, so it is per-pixel cost, not fixed overhead). This localises it.

Harness: [`examples/effort_loop_profile.rs`](../jxl-encoder/examples/effort_loop_profile.rs)
(keeps one encode looping so a sampler has something to attach to) +
macOS `sample`, 18 s at 1 ms, `RAYON_NUM_THREADS=1`, nature photo 1024² d=1 e3,
850 encodes. Self-time aggregated across all threads.

## Why XYB is expensive, and what libjxl does differently

[`xyb_cbrt_headroom`](../jxl-encoder/examples/xyb_cbrt_headroom.rs), 1 Mpx,
min of 25, single-threaded, all arms writing the same three output planes:

| arm | ms / Mpx | ns/px |
|---|--:|--:|
| shipped (`jxl_simd::linear_rgb_to_xyb_batch`) | 3.77 | 3.60 |
| scalar, our f64 two-divide Newton | 4.49 | 4.28 |
| scalar, libjxl's division-free f32 cbrt | 4.68 | 4.46 |
| matrix + bias + clamp only, no cube root | 0.42 | 0.40 |

**The cube root is 91 % of the transform.** Our `cbrt_fast` runs two
Newton iterations on the cube root directly, each with an **f64 division**,
three channels per pixel — six f64 divisions per pixel. libjxl
(`base/fast_math-inl.h::CubeRootAndAdd`) instead Newtons the INVERSE cube root
using only multiplies and FMAs, entirely in f32, and recovers `x^(1/3)` as
`r*r*x` — **no divisions at all**. Max relative disagreement between the two
over x in [1e-6, 8] is **3.0e-7**.

Two corrections to what an earlier pass of this measurement said:

1. An arm that wrote ONE output plane instead of three made the shipped SIMD
   kernel look 35 % SLOWER than scalar. It is not; with three planes it is 16 %
   faster. Do not compare arms with different store traffic.
2. libjxl's cbrt is **not** faster in scalar form — it is 4 % slower there
   (4.68 vs 4.49 ms), because four Newton iterations of multiplies beat two
   divisions only once they are in vector lanes. So this table does NOT prove
   the libjxl algorithm would be faster for us; it prices the scalar case and
   shows where the structural difference is.

## The proposal this leaves (OWNER DECISION — not taken)

`jxl-encoder-simd/src/xyb.rs::forward_xyb_impl` is nominally a
`#[magetypes(define(f32x8, f64x4), v3, neon, wasm128, scalar)]` kernel, but its
hot section leaves the vector domain: it calls `to_array()` on all three mixed
vectors, runs the bit-hack initial guess in a **scalar loop over 24 lanes**,
rebuilds six `f64x4`s with `from_array`, and reassembles through three `[f32; 8]`
staging arrays. The f64 requirement is also why the module's own comment
excludes the `v4` (AVX-512) tier — magetypes has no `F64x4Backend` for
`X64V4Token`.

Adopting libjxl's division-free f32 cube root would keep the whole kernel in
`f32x8` lanes with no array round-trip and would restore `v4` eligibility on
x86. It **moves bytes** (~3e-7 relative change in every XYB sample propagates
into quantisation), so it needs a hash-lock regeneration and an RD check, and it
is not something to land without the owner asking for it. Filed here with the
measurement so the decision has numbers attached.

Cheaper, byte-identical alternatives not yet measured: vectorising only the
initial-guess loop (pure integer bit math on the f32 lanes, same f64 Newton
afterwards), and removing the `[f32; 8]` staging arrays. Both preserve the
arithmetic exactly.
